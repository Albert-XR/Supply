If you are sourcing stainless steel cutlery for the first time, the fastest answer is this:

> **18/10** is the premium choice — more nickel, better rust resistance and shine. Choose it for hotels, restaurants and mid-to-high retail. **18/0** contains no nickel, costs noticeably less, and is strong enough for everyday use — choose it for mass retail, promotions and price-sensitive markets.

Now let's break down what those numbers actually mean, where each grade fits, and — most importantly — how to verify you got what you paid for before the goods leave the factory.

## What Do 18/0 and 18/10 Actually Mean?

The fraction describes the alloy composition of the stainless steel:

- **The first number (18)** is the percentage of **chromium (Cr)**. Chromium forms the passive oxide layer that makes steel "stainless" — it is what gives flatware its rust resistance.
- **The second number (0 or 10)** is the percentage of **nickel (Ni)**. Nickel stabilizes the austenitic structure, improves corrosion resistance, and — visibly — gives the steel its deep, long-lasting luster.

| Grade | Chromium | Nickel | Steel Type | Typical Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18/0 (430, 410) | 18% | ~0% | Ferritic / Martensitic | Budget flatware, hollow handles, knife blades |
| 18/8 (304) | 18% | 8% | Austenitic | Kitchenware, mid-range flatware |
| 18/10 | 18% | 10% | Austenitic | Premium flatware, hotel & restaurantware |

A common point of confusion: knife blades are almost always made of **410 or 420 martensitic steel** even on a full 18/10 set, because blades need hardening to hold an edge — and hardened steel cannot contain nickel. So "18/10 cutlery set" always refers to the forks and spoons, not the knives.

## Quick Comparison for Importers

| Factor | 18/0 | 18/10 |
|---|---|---|
| Material cost | Lower | ~30–50% higher |
| Rust resistance | Good (dry use) | Excellent (dishwasher safe) |
| Luster | Slightly grayish | Bright, mirror-like |
| Magnetic? | Yes | No (except knife blades) |
| Best for | Mass retail, promotions, hollow-handle sets | Hotels, restaurants, premium retail |
| MOQ positioning | Entry-level orders | Mid-to-high-end programs |

## When 18/0 Is the Right Choice

Despite the "no nickel" stigma, 18/0 flatware is a deliberate engineering trade-off, not a defect. It makes sense when:

- **Price is the deciding factor** — supermarkets, dollar stores, and promotional gift programs live on thin margins.
- **The design uses hollow handles** — hollow-handle knives and forks are lighter and cheaper, and 18/0 handles keep the set price competitive.
- **The market is dry-use oriented** — in many markets hand-washing is the norm, and 18/0 performs well for years.

One caution: 18/0 that is thin (below ~1.5mm for forks) or poorly polished will pit and rust quickly, and that is what generates customer complaints. The grade matters less than the **workmanship**.

## When 18/10 Is Worth the Premium

- **Dishwasher exposure** — nickel-rich austenitic steel withstands repeated detergent and heat cycles without graying or pitting. This is non-negotiable for hotels and restaurants.
- **Perceived value** — the brighter white-silver luster of 18/10 reads as "quality" on the table. Retail brands selling at a premium should not compromise here.
- **Long service life** — for a restaurant buying 5,000 sets, replacing a cheap set twice costs more than buying 18/10 once.

If your brand story is "affordable everyday elegance," a **hybrid strategy** works well: 18/10 forks and spoons, paired with precision-ground 410 blades — which is exactly how most professional-grade sets are built.

## Specs That Matter More Than the Grade

Experienced buyers check these before the grade itself:

1. **Thickness / weight** — forks and spoons between 2.0–3.0mm feel substantial; anything noticeably lighter bends easily.
2. **Polishing** — mirror finish should be even with no orange-peel texture or polishing-wheel marks near the tines.
3. **Edge and tine finishing** — tines should be rounded and aligned; sharp burrs are a safety recall risk.
4. **PVD coating (gold/black sets)** — quality PVD is applied by vacuum plating and passes tape and salt-spray tests; cheap spray coatings peel within weeks.
5. **Logo and packaging execution** — laser engraving depth, color-box print quality, barcode accuracy.

## How to Verify Quality Before Shipment

Regardless of grade, protect your order with these steps:

- **Salt spray test** — ask the factory to run a 48-hour neutral salt spray test on samples. A quality 18/10 set shows no rust; a good 18/0 set may show only minor spotting on cut edges.
- **Pre-shipment inspection** — inspect polish, tine alignment and plating adhesion on 2–3% of the batch (AQL 2.5 is standard for cutlery).
- **Material certificates** — request an SGS or third-party test report confirming the composition, plus **FDA / LFGB** food-contact compliance if your destination market requires it. - **The magnet trick (use with caution)** — 18/10 forks and spoons are non-magnetic; 18/0 is magnetic. Useful for a quick spot check on arrival — but remember knife blades will always attract a magnet, even on genuine 18/10 sets.

## FAQ

**Is 18/10 stainless steel dishwasher safe?**
Yes — that is one of its main advantages. Nickel stabilizes the alloy against pitting and graying from detergents and heat.

**Why is my 18/10 knife magnetic?**
Knife blades use 410/420 martensitic steel for edge retention. This is industry-standard, not a defect.

**Can I mix 18/0 and 18/10 in one set?**
Yes, and it is common: 18/10 forks and spoons with 410 blades balances cost and quality well. Even an 18/0 hollow-handle line with 18/10 solid pieces can hit a specific price point.

## The Bottom Line

| You are importing... | Choose |
|---|---|
| Supermarket / promo sets, tight unit cost | 18/0 |
| Hotel, restaurant, premium retail programs | 18/10 |
| Balanced "best value" program | 18/10 pieces + 410 blades |

The grade is a cost decision; the craftsmanship is a supplier decision. If you are comparing quotes between factories, ask for the salt spray report and pre-shipment AQL standard first — those tell you more than the "18/10" stamped on the box.

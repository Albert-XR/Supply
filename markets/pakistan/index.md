<h2>Why Pakistani Importers Buy Cutlery from China</h2>

<p>
  Pakistan's cutlery and tableware market is served largely by imports, and China is the dominant source. Three practical reasons:
</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Range.</strong> A single Chinese factory can supply mirror, satin, gold and multi-colour finishes, boxed gift sets, and both solid and hollow-handle construction — so you can build a full catalogue from one supplier instead of four.</li>
  <li><strong>Customisation.</strong> Laser-engraved logos, custom PVD colours and printed gift boxes are routine in China and let you sell under your own brand rather than competing on unbranded goods.</li>
  <li><strong>Consistency.</strong> Repeat orders can be produced to the same specification, which matters when you are supplying retail chains or hotel groups that reorder.</li>
</ul>

<p>
  Working factory-direct also means you are dealing with the people who actually run the production lines — questions about thickness, finish or packaging are answered by the factory, not relayed through a trading desk.
</p>

<h2>Quality Control Before Your Goods Leave China</h2>

<p>
  Pakistan is a long way from Guangdong — a quality problem found on arrival is expensive. Every order is handled through four checkpoints:
</p>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Sample confirmation</strong> — production follows a sample you have approved.</li>
  <li><strong>Pre-shipment inspection</strong> — finish, tine alignment, plating adhesion and packaging are checked before the goods are packed for shipping.</li>
  <li><strong>Third-party testing</strong> — independent laboratory testing can be arranged on request if your customer or your market requires a report.</li>
  <li><strong>Factory audit</strong> — you or your agent are welcome to visit, or to inspect live over video call.</li>
</ol>

<p>
  This is the part of sourcing that is worth being demanding about. If you are comparing factories, ask each one how they inspect before shipment — the answer tells you more than the sample.
</p>

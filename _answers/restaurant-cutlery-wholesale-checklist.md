## The wholesale question restaurants actually face

When a restaurant or catering company searches for cutlery wholesale, the real need is rarely "cheap spoons". It is **a predictable per-cover cost, a consistent piece in the guest's hand, and a replenishment channel that does not run dry**. That is a specification problem before it is a price problem — and it is solved by the checklist below, in order.

## 1 — Split your venue into service zones

| Zone | Duty cycle | Typical grade choice |
|---|---|---|
| Dining room (à la carte) | Moderate turns, front-of-house | Higher-lustre finish; premium operators step up grade |
| Banqueting / events | Heavy bursts, mixed handling | Durable mirror-polish 410 (18/0) |
| Room service / staff / canteen | Highest loss rate | 410 (18/0), plain pattern |

Splitting zones lets you buy the expensive feel only where guests actually see it. The rest of the operation runs on the hard-wearing grade — this is where factory-direct pricing changes your per-cover math most.

## 2 — Fix the place-setting format

- **Standard:** dinner knife + dinner fork + dinner spoon + teaspoon (a 24-piece set covers 6 covers).
- **Casual / QSR:** fork + spoon only, or wrapped two-piece rolls.
- **Banqueting:** mixed knife/fork/spoon counts per event, ordered as separate piece families rather than fixed sets.

A manufacturer that runs gift sets and hotel weight in the same plant can supply **piece families à la carte** — forks in one carton, teaspoons in another — instead of forcing you into retail box formats. Say the word "open stock" in your enquiry; factories understand it.

## 3 — Specify commercial-grade details

The differences that decide whether flatware survives a commercial dishwasher cycle:

1. **Piece weight** — hotel-weight pieces (typically 26 g+ for a dinner fork) resist bending in trays and feel right in the hand.
2. **Edge finishing** — knife serration depth and fork tine polishing determine both guest experience and wash-water drainage.
3. **Handle section** — a seamless, smooth-section handle traps no food film; ornate relief patterns look premium but raise your washing labour.
4. **Finish** — mirror polish shows water spots less than satin on most mineral-hard water; PVD gold and black finishes hold up best when the base polish is done properly.

Each of these is a line in your specification, not a hope. Send the spec, receive pre-production samples, and bend nothing in your standard.

## 4 — Plan replacement as a standing line, not an emergency

Plan on **one replacement for every three to four pieces in service**, plus a banquet buffer. Buying your replacement stock in the same production run as your launch stock keeps colour and finish matched across years — a factory can re-run the same pattern later, which a distributor sourcing opportunistically often cannot.

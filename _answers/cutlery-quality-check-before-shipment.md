## The four checks, in order

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Sample confirmation** | A sample is made to the agreed specification and approved by you before production starts |
| **Pre-shipment inspection** | Finished goods are checked against the approved sample before packing |
| **Third-party testing** | A laboratory can be arranged on request for the tests your market requires |
| **Factory audit** | On-site visits or live video inspections are welcome; see [Visit Our Factory](/visit-our-factory/) |

The approved sample is the single most useful document in this process. Once it is signed off, weight, balance, finish and print colour all have a physical reference that both sides can compare against — which is far more reliable than describing them over a message thread.

## What to check on the sample

- **Weight and balance** — pick up each piece; a set that feels light in the hand rarely sells as premium.
- **Edges and tines** — run a cloth along the fork tines and the spoon rim; there should be no burrs.
- **Finish** — check the mirror polish and any PVD colour under daylight, not just under studio lighting.
- **Engraving and print** — confirm logo depth and position, and the exact colour of any printed box or insert card.
- **Packing** — assemble the set in its box to confirm the insert holds every piece without rattling.

## Put the specification in writing

The most common first-order mistake is ordering "stainless steel cutlery" without a specification. Record at least: product type and set composition, steel grade, thickness or weight, finish, solid or hollow handle, and packaging style. If you are unsure where to start, send a photo of a product you already sell and the market you sell it in — a factory can translate that into a specification.

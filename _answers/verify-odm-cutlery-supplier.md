## Why verification matters more than the price

Most problems in cutlery sourcing — wrong steel, thin handles, mixed finishes, late delivery — trace back to one root cause: the buyer never confirmed **who actually makes the product**. A supplier that is really a trading company can quote any factory's photos and pass your audit questions to a third party. A manufacturer answers them directly.

The checklist below works for both. It is the same four-gate process experienced importers use in Jieyang, Chaozhou and Yangjiang — the three stainless steel cutlery clusters of Guangdong.

## Gate 1 — Confirm the legal factory identity

| What to ask for | What a real manufacturer provides |
|---|---|
| Business licence | Manufacturing scope covering metal / plastic products, with a factory address |
| Manufacturing address | One physical plant, matchable on a map — not an office floor |
| Manufacturer of record | The company that will be named on your production records |

**Red flag:** the "factory" address resolves to a commercial building or the supplier cannot name the plant where your order will run.

## Gate 2 — Match capacity to your order

Capacity tells you whether your delivery window is realistic before you commit to it.

- **Monthly output in pieces.** A 10 million+ pieces/month plant absorbs container-volume orders without disturbing the production schedule.
- **Headcount and lines.** 200+ workers across stamping, polishing, PVD coating and packing means each process is in-house, not outsourced to a third workshop.
- **Product range depth.** A manufacturer that already runs 24-piece gift sets, hotel-weight flatware and PVD colour finishes can switch configurations on your order — that is the "ODM" half of the relationship: you design the spec, they already run the machinery for it.

## Gate 3 — Approve physical samples

Never skip this step, whatever the order size.

1. Send your specification: set composition, steel grade, piece weight, handle type, finish, packaging style.
2. Receive pre-production samples and check **weight, balance, edge finishing and coating colour** in hand.
3. Only then release the bulk order.

Samples are available on request and refundable on bulk orders — a factory confident in its own output does not need to charge you to prove it. A photo is not a sample; mirror polish and PVD colour cannot be judged from a screen.

## Gate 4 — Lock in quality control before shipment

Agree in writing on how the order is checked before it leaves China:

- **In-line inspection** during production for critical dimensions and finish,
- **pre-shipment inspection** on packing and piece count, by the factory's own QC or a third party you appoint,
- **testing** arranged on request for market-specific requirements, through third-party labs.

Inspection records and quality documents should be available to you before the container is sealed. If the supplier resists third-party inspection, treat that as the loudest red flag of all.

## What ODM actually gives you

Once the four gates pass, ODM works in your favour: the factory already engineers the product, and your specification — logo engraving, PVD colour, insert layout, gift-box printing — rides on proven tooling. You get custom product economics without paying for custom tooling.
